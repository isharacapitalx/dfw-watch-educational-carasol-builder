# Research — Wall Street landlords selling off DFW rental homes

Topic: institutional/"Wall Street" investors, who spent years buying up DFW single-family homes, have flipped to being net sellers — accelerated by a new federal restriction on their financing access. Brand: DFW Housing Watch. Compiled 2026-07-30 from Phase 1 subagent research (Apify Instagram watchlist scan + WebSearch/WebFetch across news/institutional sources).

## Verified facts used in the script

1. President Trump signed an executive order, "Stopping Wall Street from Competing with Main Street Homebuyers," on **2026-01-20**. It restricts large institutional investors' access to federal-backed loan/securitization channels for single-family purchases; it does not force any existing portfolio sale; build-to-rent development is exempted. — Source: multiple law-firm client alerts (Jones Day, Manatt, Greenberg Traurig, Dechert, Brownstein), retrieved 2026-07-30.
2. "Wall Street is selling more rental homes, as buying ban takes effect." — CNBC, 2026-07-21. https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/21/wall-street-selling-homes-real-estate.html (retrieved 2026-07-30)
3. Dallas-area institutional investors own **9.2%** of the housing stock but account for **22.8%** of new for-sale listings (Parcl Labs data). — cited via CNBC 2026-07-21 and The Real Deal, "Wall Street Landlords Flip To Selling Amid Housing Law," 2026-07-22 (retrieved 2026-07-30).
4. FirstKey Homes is listing roughly 2x the volume of peer institutional investors in Dallas, cutting prices ~10% off original list, and repricing listings roughly every 20 days. — Parcl Labs data via The Real Deal, 2026-07-22 (retrieved 2026-07-30).
5. "D-FW has the most investor purchases, of any kind, of any U.S. metro" — Hannah Jones, Realtor.com senior economic research analyst, via NBC5/Dallas Morning News, "New federal housing law to restrict mega investors. What presence do they have in D-FW?" (retrieved 2026-07-30). https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/local/dallas-morning-news/new-federal-housing-law-to-restrict-mega-investors-what-presence-do-they-have-in-d-fw/4048586/
6. In Fort Worth, roughly **26%** of the city's 247,400 single-family homes (64,372 properties) are owned by a company/investor entity (LLC/LTD/INC/Trust — identified by no homestead exemption on file), not an individual homeowner. — Fort Worth Report, "26% of Fort Worth's single-family homes are commercially owned," 2024-06-04 (retrieved 2026-07-30). https://fortworthreport.org/2024/06/04/26-of-fort-worths-single-family-homes-are-commercially-owned/ ; also WFAA, "Fort Worth, Texas: Quarter of homes owned by businesses" (retrieved 2026-07-30).
7. Large institutional investors nationally have already been net sellers of single-family rental homes for some time, pivoting toward build-to-rent development instead of existing-home acquisition. — CNBC, "Big investors have been fleeing for-sale housing market, even before Trump ordered ban," 2026-03-04 (retrieved 2026-07-30).

## How to identify an investor-owned listing (used for slide 7, actionable/public-record info, not advice)

- Owner of record is an LLC, LTD, INC, or Trust rather than an individual name.
- No homestead exemption filed on the property (public county appraisal district record) — a reliable proxy used by Fort Worth's own city analysis (source #6 above) to identify non-owner-occupied/investor ownership.
- Extended days-on-market and repeat price reductions on the listing — publicly visible on MLS-syndicated listing sites.

These are all facts a buyer can check for free in public county appraisal district records and on any listing site — no proprietary data or paid service implied.
